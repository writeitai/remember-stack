1"""What the signed-credential perimeter trusts, and how it stays current (D136 §7.5). 

2 

3Two documents published by the key issuer decide whether a signed credential 

4can be accepted at all: 

5 

6- the **key set** (a JWKS: the issuer's Ed25519 public keys, each named by a 

7 ``kid``), fetched from ``API_SIGNING_KEYS_URL``; 

8- the **revocation document**, a JWS the issuer signs for this one deployment 

9 and re-issues every refresh interval R. It lists the credential ids 

10 (``revoked``) refused despite a valid signature and the signing-key ids 

11 (``active_kids``) whose credentials remain valid. 

12 

13This module fetches both every R, decides whether a newly fetched revocation 

14document may replace the one already accepted, persists the accepted document 

15in the spine so a restart cannot roll revocation back, and answers the one 

16question every request asks: *is there a fresh accepted document right now, 

17and with which keys?* 

18 

19## Acceptance 

20 

21- The first document on a deployment with nothing accepted is accepted when 

22 its signature verifies against the fetched key set and its ``iss`` and 

23 ``aud`` (this deployment's id) match. 

24- Every later document must also be signed by a ``kid`` that is active in the 

25 previously accepted document and carry a strictly greater ``seq``. The same 

26 ``seq`` with identical claims is a no-op; a lower ``seq``, or the same one 

27 with different claims, is a rollback attempt: rejected and logged. 

28 

29The signer rule is what makes key rotation safe to rely on: once the issuer 

30drops a ``kid`` from ``active_kids``, a stolen private key of that generation 

31cannot sign a document that brings itself back. 

32 

33## Freshness 

34 

35A document is fresh until ``min(exp, iat + S)``. Without a fresh 

36document — before the first one is accepted, or once the accepted one ages 

37out, including one loaded from the spine at start-up that is already old — 

38every signed credential is refused. A key revoked at time *r* therefore stops 

39working by *r + S + leeway* (the 30 s credential clock tolerance) whatever 

40happens to fetches. 

41 

42## Several API replicas 

43 

44Each replica refreshes on its own, but they share the persisted row. A 

45refresh starts from whichever is newer, its own copy or the persisted one, and 

46the write is a compare-and-set on the stored ``seq`` the document was 

47verified against. A replica that loses the race reloads the row and verifies 

48its candidate again — including the signer rule against the peer's document — 

49so one replica can neither move the deployment backwards nor bring back a 

50signing key a peer's document retired. A document is published to 

51requests only after it is persisted. 

52"""
