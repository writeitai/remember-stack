# Data handling and security

<AppliesTo products={["remember.dev"]} />

This page says where your memory lives, how it is kept apart from other
customers', what passes through remember.dev's own services, and what is
not promised. Read the last section before you send anything you cannot
replace.

## Isolation

Each project has its own deployment. A deployment has:

- its own PostgreSQL database;
- its own storage buckets for source files and derived artifacts;
- its own credentials and signing key;
- its own model provider key;
- its own hostname, `https://<deployment-id>.dp.remember.dev`.

Deployments share physical hosts, but not databases, buckets or
credentials. There are no tenant rows inside one shared engine, and
projects inside the same organisation are isolated from each other as
strictly as projects of different customers.

A deployment API token names exactly one deployment. Another deployment
refuses it.

## How requests reach your deployment

Your client connects straight to your deployment's hostname. The
`*.dp.remember.dev` addresses point at a remember.dev gateway that routes
the connection by hostname without decrypting it; TLS ends inside your
deployment. The gateway holds no certificate or key for your hostname.

The console and the account API (`https://remember.dev/app/api`) handle
sign-in, tokens, projects and billing. They do not relay your memory
requests. When the console shows your memory, your browser fetches it from
the deployment directly with a 10-minute, read-only credential.

**Hosted MCP is the exception.** When an agent uses
[hosted MCP](/docs/cloud/hosted-mcp), the tool call and its result pass through
Cloudflare and the account API on their way to and from your deployment.
remember.dev does not store them.

## What remember.dev's control plane holds

The control plane keeps account and billing data: your email address, name
and password hash (or Google link), organisations, members, projects,
token metadata, the credit ledger, statements and support records.

For billing it receives measurements from each deployment: character
counts, byte counts, timestamps, outcomes and opaque identifiers. These
measurements carry no document text, file names or content hashes.

Tokens are stored only as hashes. A token's secret is shown once and
cannot be recovered.

## Where data lives

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Deployments: databases, pipeline workers | Hetzner, Falkenstein, Germany |
| Source files and artifacts | A remember.dev storage cell at Hetzner, Falkenstein, Germany |
| Deployment gateway | Hetzner, Falkenstein, Germany |
| Secrets, the billing meter service and fleet automation | Google Cloud, `europe-west1` (Belgium) |
| Website, DNS and the console's front door | Cloudflare |

remember.dev runs in this one location. There is no second region and no
replication.

## Outside services

| Service | What it receives | Why |
|---|---|---|
| OpenRouter, and the model providers it routes to | Document text and questions, as model input, while the pipeline processes documents and answers retrieval that uses models | Language models and embeddings |
| Stripe | Your billing email, payment details and the amounts you buy | Payments and tax |
| Google | Your sign-in, if you use Google sign-in | Authentication |
| Brevo | Your email address and the transactional message | Verification, reset and invitation emails |
| Cloudflare | Traffic to the website, the console and the account API, including hosted MCP calls. Not traffic to your deployment's hostname | DNS, the website and the console's front door |

Each project has its own OpenRouter key with its own spending limit.

## Diagnostics

Diagnostics are on by default. When a deployment reports problems for
remember.dev to investigate, those diagnostics can include the text of
model inputs and outputs. Error reports and logs are kept without content.

You can opt out for your organisation under
**Settings → Privacy & diagnostics**. Opting out is an owner setting.

Diagnostics are held on an observability host that remember.dev runs.
Diagnostics with content are deleted after 30 days; logs and error reports
after 90 days.

## Records of what happened

remember.dev writes an append-only record of account actions: tokens
minted, presented and revoked, device logins approved, projects created and
archived, members added, invitations, billing decisions and offboarding
steps. The record names who acted, or which token.

The credit ledger is append-only too; see [Pricing and credits](/docs/cloud/pricing).

You cannot read or download these records yet. If you need to know what
happened on your account, ask [support](/docs/cloud/support).

## Support access

remember.dev does not yet have a way for you to grant support staff
time-limited access to your deployment, and the console has no support
access to approve. Support works from what you tell them in a support
record, from the account records above, and from diagnostics.

## Token hygiene

- Give each machine or job its own deployment token, with a label that
  names it.
- Keep tokens in environment variables or a secret manager. Never commit
  one, and never paste one into an agent's chat; connect agents with
  [hosted MCP](/docs/cloud/hosted-mcp).
- Rotate tokens before they expire; the console and the CLI warn you.
- If a token leaks, revoke it first under **Settings → API Tokens**, then
  mint a replacement.
- `remember logout` revokes the key stored on a machine you are giving up.

Details are in [Tokens and sign-in](/docs/cloud/tokens-and-sign-in).

## What remember.dev does not promise

- **No backups.** remember.dev does not promise that your data is backed up
  or can be restored. A failed host or disk can lose it.
- **No export.** There is no export or download of your memory, now or
  later. Keep your own copies of your source files. They are also how you
  move to a self-hosted deployment: re-ingest them there.
- **No availability or latency figures.** There is no service level
  agreement.
- **No certifications** and no single sign-on.
- **No erasure of a single document.** Deleting a document removes it from
  the memory, but its stored original and its claims stay in the project's
  storage as history until the project is closed.
- **No per-document permissions.** Anyone who can read a project's memory
  can read all of it. Put material that needs a different audience in a
  different project.

For personal-data requests under the GDPR, write to privacy@remember.dev.
To report a security problem, write to security@remember.dev.
