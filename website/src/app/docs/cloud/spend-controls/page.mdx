# Spend caps and auto top-up

<AppliesTo products={["remember.dev"]} />

remember.dev cannot spend more than you have paid for. On top of that you
can set how much it may spend in a month, for the whole organisation and
for each project. When a limit is reached, chargeable work stops before it
starts; nothing is billed after the fact.

## The organisation's monthly cap

An owner sets the cap under **Billing → Monthly hard cap**, in credits.

- The month is the UTC calendar month.
- Before any chargeable work, remember.dev checks that this month's
  charges, plus the holds still open, plus the new work's hold stay within
  the cap. If they would not, the work does not start.
- Without a cap, spending is limited only by the balance.

You can set a first cap and lower it at any time. **Raising or removing a
cap needs remember.dev's approval**: the request is refused with `403`, and
you ask [support](/docs/cloud/support) to make the change.

If you set a cap below what your active projects need for the rest of the
day, they stop at the next daily funding point.

## Project sub-caps

An owner can give each project its own monthly limit (a sub-cap), through
the API:

```bash
curl -s -X PUT \
  "https://remember.dev/app/api/v1/orgs/$ORG_ID/projects/$PROJECT_ID/spend-cap" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  --cookie "__session=..." \
  -d '{"monthly_subcap_credits": "50"}'
```

Send `null` to remove it. Owners can raise, lower or remove a sub-cap
without approval.

A sub-cap works like the organisation cap, counted for that project only.
When a project reaches its sub-cap, only that project stops; the others
carry on while the organisation has balance and headroom under its own
cap.

## What stops when money or a cap runs out

Three different things can be refused, and they behave differently.

**Processing new documents.** Each new document version needs a hold for
its processing before the expensive work starts. If the hold is refused,
the document waits unprocessed; it has not failed. When you add credit or
raise the limit, processing continues.

**The project itself.** An active project needs a hold for the rest of the
current UTC day, placed at the start of each day. If the balance or a cap
cannot cover it, the project is parked:

- no new work starts: new documents wait unprocessed, and nothing fails;
- the active-project charge stops at the same moment;
- your memory stays readable: search, the assured operations and SQL
  queries keep answering;
- your data, tokens and hostname are kept.

When a purchase or a higher limit makes the next hold possible, the parked
work resumes on the same hostname with the same memory and the same tokens.

<Callout type="warning">

Today a parked project goes further than this: its deployment is
**blocked** and the endpoint stops answering all requests, reads
included, until the project is funded again. This will change so that
reads continue. Until then, keep enough balance for at least a day of
every active project, or turn on auto top-up.

</Callout>

**Storage growth.** Each new document version also places a small hold for
the storage it will add. If a project's daily storage measurement is
missing for 48 hours, the project stops accepting data that would grow its
storage until measurement resumes; reads continue. You are not charged
for that period.

### Provider-cost safeguard

Separately from your credits, every deployment has a ceiling on what its
model provider calls may cost, set by remember.dev. It protects both you
and remember.dev from a runaway pipeline. If it is reached, the deployment
refuses ingest, search, operations and SQL queries with `423` and a
`detail` starting `dispatch_parked:`, or with `403` and `dispatch_refused:`.
Contact [support](/docs/cloud/support) if you see either.

The spend gate's current decision is readable at
`GET /v1/orgs/{org_id}/deployments/{deployment_id}/spend-safety/gate`, and
`remember-status` includes it.

## Warnings

remember.dev does not yet send a warning as you approach a cap or run low
on balance. Watch **Billing** and **Usage**, use `remember-status` in a
scheduled job, or turn on auto top-up.

## Auto top-up

Auto top-up buys credits for you when the balance runs low, within a
monthly ceiling you set. It is off by default, and only an owner can turn
it on.

### Turn it on

1. Buy credits once with **Save card for auto top-up** ticked. That saves
   the card with Stripe for later charges.
2. Under **Billing → Auto top-up**, set:
    - **Threshold:** the balance, in whole credits, below which a top-up is
      bought (default 5);
    - **Amount:** how many credits each top-up buys, within the same minimum
      and maximum as a manual purchase (default 25);
    - **Monthly ceiling:** the most auto top-up may charge in a calendar
      month, in euros (default 200). It must be at least the amount.
3. Enable it.

If the card cannot be saved or billing is not active yet, the card says so
and cannot be enabled. If it says auto top-up is unavailable, it cannot be
turned on for your organisation yet.

### What it does

When the balance is below the threshold, remember.dev charges the saved card
for the amount, without you present. The credits are added when Stripe
confirms the payment, exactly as for a manual purchase.

It does not charge when:

- the charge would take the month's auto top-ups over the ceiling;
- another top-up for the organisation is already in progress;
- the last attempt was less than 60 seconds ago;
- billing is not active, or no card is saved.

### When a charge fails

- If the bank asks for you to confirm the payment (strong customer
  authentication), auto top-up turns itself off at once. Buy credits
  manually, which saves the card again, and turn it back on.
- After three failed charges in a row it turns itself off.
- A successful charge resets the count.

A charge whose outcome Stripe has not reported is not retried
automatically; remember.dev reconciles it first so you are never charged
twice.

Auto top-up only adds credits. It does not raise your monthly cap, so a
project can still stop at the cap with a healthy balance.
