## P1

1. **`plan/designs/postgres_p1_search_projection_design.md:277` — The BM25 equivalence claim excludes rows today's full scan returns.** The existing lexical statements order all eligible rows by the standalone operator and ID, without requiring a positive score (`src/rememberstack/adapters/postgres_p1.py:900`, also claims at 731). Thus a query with no matching term can return `k` zero-score rows; a query with fewer than `k` matches can include a zero-score tail. The index scans matching postings and excludes zero-score candidates; this is visible in [v1.5.1's BMW implementation](https://github.com/timescale/pg_textsearch/blob/v1.5.1/src/scoring/bmw.c#L1580-L1586). Refill cannot recover rows absent from that stream. This also makes the small exact-scoring tier and large index tier disagree, and changes RRF contributions, not just exposed raw scores.

   **Concrete fix:** Choose and bind the zero-score contract. To preserve full-scan behavior, specify a bounded, stable-ID eligibility walk that fills the zero-score tail after positive matches are exhausted. Alternatively explicitly adopt positive-match-only lexical semantics across every path, revise the equivalence claim and affected operation contracts, and gate the resulting answer-quality change. Test absent terms, empty analyzed queries, fewer-than-`k` matches, and both sides of the exact-tier threshold.

2. **`plan/designs/postgres_p1_search_projection_design.md:256` — `REPEATABLE READ` does not freeze the BM25 scoring generation across the proposed repeated queries.** The proof assumes every larger `LIMIT` is another prefix of one immutable ranking. In v1.5.1, `tp_scoring_snapshot_get` caches corpus statistics/segment state in the executor's query context, not the PostgreSQL transaction snapshot. A subsequent statement captures them again. Concurrent ingestion can therefore change IDF/length statistics between rounds even though authority rows remain repeatable; accumulated scores, tie boundaries, and the next-score stopping proof can become inconsistent. This is source-level evidence from [snapshot.c](https://github.com/timescale/pg_textsearch/blob/v1.5.1/src/scoring/snapshot.c#L128-L215), reinforced by the upstream test distinguishing refreshed statement executions from a [cursor that retains its generation across FETCH](https://github.com/timescale/pg_textsearch/blob/v1.5.1/test/scripts/standalone_snapshot.sh#L305-L341).

   **Concrete fix:** Specify a stable scoring stream, for example one server cursor/executor per BM25 nomination with bounded fetches, and carry its scores through confirmation without rescoring under another generation. Explain continuation and tie lookahead precisely, including how the extension's internal refill behaves. Add an isolated concurrency test that inserts term-skewed documents and spills/compacts between fetches; exactness must hold for one defined scoring generation as well as one authority snapshot.

3. **`plan/designs/postgres_p1_search_projection_design.md:261` — A short HNSW batch is incorrectly declared exhausted.** Section 8.2 ends on fewer rows than requested, while §9 at 330 requires truncation when rows remain unvisited. The latter supplies no mechanism for establishing that condition. Raising `hnsw.max_scan_tuples` to a round's output size does not prove completion: it bounds visited graph tuples approximately, does not bound the initial scan, and memory is another stopping condition. Strict order can also discard subsequently discovered closer candidates. These behaviors are explicit in the [0.8.6 documentation](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector/tree/v0.8.6#iterative-scan-options) and [scan implementation](https://github.com/pgvector/pgvector/blob/v0.8.6/src/hnswscan.c#L247-L318). Splitting confirmation into a second query does not expose the missing stop reason.

   **Concrete fix:** Define channel-specific completion rules. Treat a short ANN stream as incomplete unless a specified, budgeted mechanism independently proves exhaustion of the searchable set; otherwise disclose truncation or return a typed boundary. Account for both traversal and memory caps, null/zero vectors, attestation filters, and repeated-round work. Test a cap and a memory stop before the adapter's nominated-candidate budget is reached, separately from genuine exhaustion. The quality gate covers approximation; it cannot substitute for honest termination metadata.

4. **`plan/analysis/index_driven_ranked_search.md:315` — The existing bridge does not automatically inherit one shared snapshot.** The design says existing signatures and bridge calls suffice. In fact, the adapter opens its own SQLAlchemy connections (`postgres_p1.py:1617`), whereas the public SQL bridge subsequently confirms those nominations on the executor's separate psycopg connection (`surfaces/query_sandbox/bridge.py:345` and 364). `open_query_space_design.md:980` still requires nomination confirmation and the caller statement to share one snapshot. A newer adapter snapshot can nominate `k` newly inserted rows that the older caller snapshot rejects, losing older valid results without refill. Section 8.4 also needs to say whether both fact branches share the invocation's snapshot rather than each owning one.

   **Concrete fix:** Define ownership of a ranked-read transaction/context and how the bridge and compound reads reuse it or import its exported snapshot. Include readiness checks, all refill rounds, coverage tiers, both fact branches, and final authority confirmation. Pin the evaluation/belief instants once too; a stable MVCC snapshot alone does not freeze `statement_timestamp()` across statements. Preserve the independent-channel allowance only where no shared caller snapshot is promised, and specify which instant `pg_confirmed_at` reports. Add concurrent-insert/delete and effective-boundary tests through the actual public SQL bridge.

5. **`plan/designs/postgres_p1_search_projection_design.md:301` — The fact branch merge drops coverage-first ranking.** “Distance, then qualifier and ID, as before” is false for entity-scoped facts. Both current fact methods order branches and their combined result by `coverage DESC` before distance (`postgres_p1.py:1218`, 1228, and 1334 onward). Section 8.3 derives coverage from mentions, which is suitable for testimony but does not specify fact endpoint coverage. With anchors A and B and `k=1`, the proposed distance merge can prefer a closer A-only observation over an A–B relation, violating D87.

   **Concrete fix:** Bind fact coverage tiers using survivor-resolved subjects/endpoints, preserve the distinction between eligible `entity_ids` and `ranking_entity_ids`, and merge by `(coverage DESC, distance, fact_kind, fact_id)`. Apply any branch depth only after that ordering and eligibility; use exact scoring for bounded fact tiers where appropriate. Test two-anchor versus one-anchor results across both tables, redirected endpoints, graph-expanded eligible scopes, and overlapping relation/observation UUIDs.

6. **`plan/designs/postgres_p1_search_projection_design.md:408` — Truncation has no defined path from the adapter to callers.** The proposed loop promises nominated/eligible/drop counters and a stop reason, but analysis §6.6 preserves existing signatures. Those return only `tuple[P1Nomination, ...]`; `P1Nomination` has no invocation metadata. An empty capped search is consequently indistinguishable from an exhausted one to `_scored_nomination_envelope` (`query_engine.py:4090`), which currently manufactures `known_empty`. The unchanged SRF bridge likewise records only the final nominations, hiding the rejected refill work. Replaying the loop for `scope_pending` creates the same issue: a bounded probe cannot silently support the existing promise that its count is exact.

   **Concrete fix:** Specify a private structured search result carrying rows plus termination reason, work/drop counters, effective limits, and snapshot/clock attestation, including zero-row results. Update the internal port/bridge/engine contract in the design and delivery plan. Define propagation through RRF, per-fact-branch merges, combined envelopes, and `QueryResult.semantic_invocations`; truncation must survive empty outer SQL results. A capped pending probe must disclose incompleteness through an explicitly revised contract or fail with a boundary, never report an exact empty count. Add end-to-end empty-cap and one-channel-cap fixtures.

7. **`plan/designs/postgres_p1_search_projection_design.md:248` — The nomination SQL is still cost-planned; index usage is not guaranteed by removing joins.** A single-table `WHERE deployment_id … ORDER BY distance LIMIT n` can use a sequential scan plus sort, especially for small tables, selective deployment filters, or a growing `n`. The proposed extension's own [index-usage instructions](https://github.com/timescale/pg_textsearch/blob/v1.5.1/README.md#verifying-index-usage) explicitly acknowledge this. Therefore §9's claim that plans do not depend on estimates and a fixture-only plan-shape assertion do not establish the property on which this fix depends. The final, large refill can recreate the original full scoring pass.

   **Concrete fix:** Specify and verify the mechanism that enforces the admitted nomination plan, or explicitly retain cost-based planning with bounded exact paths and an honest performance contract. If narrowly scoped plan controls are required, compare them for nomination alone; they need not affect authority probes as in rejected option A. Cover all generated query forms, prepared/custom plans, small/large corpora, and late refill limits. An unexpected runtime plan needs a defined bounded response, rather than an unsupported claim that it cannot occur.

## P2

1. **`plan/designs/postgres_p1_search_projection_design.md:472` — `k/s` bounds candidate output, not corpus-independent search cost.** If only a fixed small eligible set remains while invisible/superseded rows grow, `s` shrinks as `1/N`, so nomination is linear until capped. Equal-score runs can span the corpus even with `s=1`; stable-ID exactness requires reading the whole boundary run. WAND posting traversal and entity coverage aggregation are additional work beyond nominated IDs. Distinct-text indexing does not remove equal-score texts with different embedding contexts. A flat latency curve obtained by returning progressively less complete results is not the promised scale result.

   **Concrete fix:** State the common-case assumptions and the linear/adversarial cases in the binding design and D142 consequences. Benchmark fixed and declining eligible fractions, long ties, superseded editions, rare entities and hubs, with the proposal disabled. Report returned-`k` rate, truncation rate, recall, nomination/mention/index work, and latency together. Define admission/termination for an exceeded mention-enumeration budget before trusting partial coverage counts. Keep the existing 4k, 5,000 and candidate-budget values explicitly as starting points to measure, as the PR already does.

2. **`plan/proposals/distinct_text_search_index.md:70` — The unchosen proposal changes chunk grain while claiming identical answers.** D140's representative plus `matching_editions` rule belongs to `search_documents`, not chunk search. Chunk search returns separate selected version/chunk identities, and overlapping declarations can select multiple editions even for `current`/`at`. Collapsing them to one representative changes `k`, citations, stable-ID ties and RRF membership. `overlap` is not specified at all. In the worked history example, today's result has separate chunk identities for the nine A editions, rather than one A hit containing a new editions field.

   **Concrete fix:** Preserve the proposal's unchosen status, but fan each nominated text entry out to every eligible chunk identity before the chunk relevance cut, with coverage and stable-ID ordering at member grain. Reserve lineage/edition aggregation for `search_documents`. Budget all examined membership rows, not merely returned entries, and test history, overlap, concurrent editions, duplicate chunks and deletion. Any deliberate new chunk result grain requires its own API/design decision. BM25 statistics changes also need lexical/hybrid quality measurements before adoption; “slightly” and “more faithful” are not established properties.

3. **`plan/plans/index_driven_ranked_search.md:33` — The pilot trigger is circular and does not isolate duplication.** The owner must decide before WP-RS.2 using candidates examined by D142's loop, but no pilot/prototype work package produces that measurement before the loop exists. Candidates per returned result also includes deletions, entity/document/time rejection and batch overfetch. A factor of five can occur with no mergeable inputs, so it does not by itself justify this particular schema change; a high value could remain high after deduplication.

   **Concrete fix:** Add a sequenced isolated pilot/prototype prerequisite, with its dataset, query set and measurement artifacts. Record rejection reasons and distinct indexed-input keys, and estimate or measure the work eliminated by sharing entries. Retain median ≥5 / extrapolated p95 as owner-confirmed investigation thresholds, but require demonstrated latency/completeness benefit and quality gates before adoption, with binding corpus reconciliation in that adopting change. LegalIt can motivate this decision without becoming an engine-specific runtime policy.

4. **`plan/designs/postgres_p1_search_projection_design.md:438` — The binding corpus still contains contrary current contracts.** This file still says scale/paid benchmarks are excluded and “benchmarking is not” required, contradicting new §9; §14 still promises filtering in one SQL statement at 453. `retrieval_design.md:172` and `open_query_space_design.md:72`, 628 and 721 retain same-statement P1 rules. `p2_graph_design.md:614` still requires the old 1.3.1 compatibility-patch pin. The D94/D98 original-entry amendment notes are correctly present, but D140's original ranked-statement clause (`decisions.md:6783`) has no corresponding refinement note. The P1 design also retains delivery sequencing in §13 despite the repository's explicit placement rule.

   **Concrete fix:** Reconcile every affected binding paragraph and schema comment, including the PG19 image section, rather than relying on the new sections to override them. Move delivery sequencing to `plan/plans/` and label historical cutover evidence as historical. Add the narrow D142 refinement inside D140's original entry while preserving its scope, occurrence, evidence-gate and pending semantics.

5. **`plan/analysis/index_driven_ranked_search.md:264` — Several external/performance assertions remain unverified, and the quality gate is not reproducible.** The generic 95–99% HNSW recall range and “random vectors are a known worst case” are not backed by a cited experiment or authoritative source for this index/configuration. The benchmark JSONs support the reported synthetic timings, not the opening claim about every ranked statement on every real deployment. “A full scan is the only correct plan” at 106 also overlooks exact scoring of a selectively prefiltered subset. Option A at 158 attributes silent caps to both extensions after upgrading BM25, despite the selected 1.5.1 refill behavior. PG19 support is [best effort with nonblocking CI](https://github.com/timescale/pg_textsearch/blob/v1.5.1/README.md#postgresql-version-compatibility), so compatibility on the pinned image remains a local gate, not an established upstream guarantee.

   **Concrete fix:** Qualify these as hypotheses or remove them, link immutable primary sources, and update the alternatives comparison under the selected versions. Keep WP-RS.0's real-data EXPLAIN check before adapter implementation. Define a measured recall acceptance threshold, representative query strata (including filters, period scopes and hubs), and a stated statistical comparison for LoCoMo; “beyond run-to-run noise” alone is not an executable gate. Keep BM25 oracle comparisons on the same extension/scoring generation, and separately measure the upgrade against today's answers.

6. **`plan/analysis/index_driven_ranked_search.md:380` — The deletion-between-rounds test expects the opposite of the stated snapshot boundary.** A row eligible at the snapshot remains visible to that read if its deletion commits later. The parenthetical “invisible: the snapshot predates it” can make the acceptance test demand disappearance between rounds, contradicting §6.5 and the binding design. [PostgreSQL's repeatable-read contract](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/19/transaction-iso.html#XACT-REPEATABLE-READ) supports the binding wording.

   **Concrete fix:** Split the fixture: deletion committed before snapshot acquisition is absent; deletion committed between rounds does not alter that invocation's authority view, and is absent on the next invocation. Assert consistent nomination/confirmation and reported authority time in both cases.
